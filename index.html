Made, not mass-produced

Aurum is Latin for gold. We chose it because good technology should be treated like fine work: planned carefully, built with care and made to last.

We are a small studio, so every customer deals with the person doing the work. We explain what we did in plain words and show you how to look after it.

We also publish tutorials and build videos, and sell guides and kits for people who want to make things themselves.

Our USP is simple: one trusted studio for PCs, electronics, 3D printing and private AI. Our core competency is hands-on hardware skill. Our target market is hobbyists, students, creators and small businesses, and our competitive advantage is that you deal directly with the person doing the work.

Business words, explained

The plain-English terms we use when we talk about how Aurum Labs runs.

Money

ROI (return on investment)
The gain you get from money you put in, compared with what it cost.
Break-even point
The sales level where income equals costs. No profit and no loss.
Cash flow
Money coming in and going out over a period of time. It keeps the business running day to day.
Accounts payable (AP)
Money the business owes to its suppliers.
Accounts receivable (AR)
Money customers owe the business for work already delivered.
Profit margin
How much of each pound of sales is left after costs.

Strategy

USP (unique selling point)
The one thing that makes a product or brand different from the rest.
Competitive advantage
An edge over rivals, such as lower costs or better technology.
Target market
The specific group of people or businesses the products are made for.
Scalability
The ability to grow sales without costs growing at the same speed.
Core competency
The main skills and know-how that make an organisation strong.

Working life

Bandwidth
The time and energy someone has for new tasks.
Synergy
When teams working together achieve more than they would alone.
Deliverable
A real output that must be finished, such as a report or a product.
Stakeholder
Anyone with an interest in how the business does.
Outsource
Paying an outside company to do part of the work.
